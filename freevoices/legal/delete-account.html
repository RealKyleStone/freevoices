Deleting your FreeVoices account

You can close your FreeVoices account and have your personal information deleted or anonymised at any time. You do not need a reason, and it costs nothing.

Option 1 — in the app (fastest)

  1. Sign in to FreeVoices.
  2. Go to Settings → Privacy & data.
  3. Choose Close my account.
  4. Confirm with your password.

Before you do, you may want to choose Download my data on the same screen. It gives you a machine-readable copy of your account, your customers, your documents and your payment records. Once the deletion completes we cannot restore it.

Option 2 — by email

If you cannot sign in, or you have already uninstalled the app, email admin@madeoc.co.za with the subject Account deletion request, from the email address on the account.

Tell us the email address of the account you want closed. We may ask you to verify your identity before we act — this protects you from someone else deleting your records. We will confirm in writing once it is done.

What happens next

WhenWhat happens
ImmediatelyYour account is closed. You are signed out everywhere, all active sessions are destroyed, and any recurring invoices stop. You can no longer sign in.
Within 30 daysWe delete or irreversibly anonymise your personal information — your name, email address, telephone number, address, company registration and VAT numbers, banking details, password, uploaded logo, and your customers' contact details.
Within 30 daysEncrypted backups containing your data reach the end of their 30-day lifecycle and are automatically purged.

Changed your mind?

For 30 days after closing your account you can reactivate it by signing in with your existing password. After 30 days the anonymisation runs and cannot be reversed.

What we have to keep, and why

We cannot delete everything, and we would rather be straight with you about it than promise otherwise.

South African law requires that records of issued invoices, credit notes and payments be kept for 7 years — the Companies Act 71 of 2008, the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 and the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991. POPIA section 14(1)(a) permits us to keep information where another law requires it.

So after anonymisation, what remains of a closed account is the financial skeleton of documents you issued: document numbers, dates, amounts, VAT and line-item descriptions. The personal information attached to them — your identity, your contact details, your banking details, your customers' contact details — is removed, so those records can no longer be linked back to you or to them.

Access to anything we retain is restricted, and it is used only for the reason it is retained. It is never used for marketing, analytics, product development or profiling.

If a legal claim, a tax audit, a fraud investigation or a complaint to the Information Regulator concerns your information, we may have to keep it for longer, until that matter is finally resolved. We will tell you if that applies to you, unless we are legally prohibited from doing so.
